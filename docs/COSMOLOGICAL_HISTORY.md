# Fenumion — Cosmological History

This dossier reconciles the 26-event cosmological spine supplied on 23 September 2026 with the existing Codex chronology. It is a connective history, not a claim that every metaphysical mechanism is resolved.

## Evidence rule

Fenumion contains objective cosmology, incomplete mortal knowledge, interested divine testimony, factional doctrine, and later interpretation. These layers must remain distinct. A god's explanation is evidence from a powerful witness; it is not automatically Ada's voice or objective truth.

## The central structure

Ada creates free persons and divine caretakers. The caretakers possess real power but do not own other wills. Divine conflict corrupts Etz Chaim's Sap, creating the material associated with Delerium and damaging reality. Death gains access and benefits from the catastrophe. The gods complete the Shattering as a desperate firebreak. Mortals inherit a broken world and morally non-final divine authorities. Death offers surrender and finality; Hope preserves possibility. Mortals eventually acquire enough power to face the same stewardship test the gods failed.

The resulting question is not only **Can this be saved?** It is:

**What are you allowed to do to someone else in order to save it?**

## 1. Ada creates reality and genuinely free persons

**Era:** Primordial  
**Confidence:** Established

Ada is the ultimate Creator layer. The beings mortals call gods are created caretakers beneath Ada. Mortals and other persons possess genuine wills.

The fundamental consequences are:

- power does not create permission;
- caretaking is not ownership;
- a will belongs to the person whose will it is;
- freely choosing to bear a cost is morally different from being selected as someone else's price.

## 2. The divine caretakers receive domains

**Era:** Primordial  
**Confidence:** Structure established; hierarchy incomplete

Known entrusted domains include Cala over Light, Fire, and Battle; Nuru over Night, Wisdom, and Cunning; Physisia over Nature, Order, and Change; Aionia over Time and Space; Mya over Hope; Namo'o over Souls; Talan over Justice and Order; and Moirah over Fate and events.

A caretaker can fail, disagree, hide information, become ashamed, or misuse delegated authority. Talan tends Justice without becoming justice itself. Physisia tends Nature without owning every living being.

## 3. Etz Chaim embodies life's original order

**Era:** Primordial  
**Confidence:** High; exact ontology incomplete

Etz Chaim, the One Tree, belongs to the original order of life and pure goodness. It is not Ada. Later Trees, Shards, Hope, Gael, restoration, and corruption echo or intersect with it, but the archive does not force those resemblances into a single metaphysical family tree.

## 4. Divine conflict corrupts the Sap

**Era:** Primordial catastrophe  
**Confidence:** Broad sequence established

Divine rivalry turns life's source into an object of conflict. Nuru and Cala learn that the Sap can be corrupted into a vessel or weapon; Death enables knowledge of that corruption. The resulting material becomes associated with amber Delerium.

Delerium is therefore not merely powerful fuel. It is corrupted creation, a theological wound, and a link between divine war and later mortal catastrophe.

## 5. Reality cracks and Death gains access

**Era:** Primordial catastrophe  
**Confidence:** High

The corruption damages reality itself. Dumuzi gains access to the world's structure, the Void becomes part of shattered creation, and Death feeds dead gods to the Wyrm.

Dumuzi can truthfully indict the gods' failures while omitting his own causal role. Provenance matters precisely because a hostile witness may still tell substantial truth.

## 6. The gods complete the Shattering as a firebreak

**Era:** Primordial catastrophe  
**Confidence:** High

After reality is already damaged, the surviving gods deliberately extend or complete the Shattering. Regions, Shards, Trees, gods, and survivors are separated or hidden to slow Death and preserve what remains.

The caretakers are both culprits and emergency responders. Neither a wholly malicious-gods account nor an innocent-gods-versus-Death account is sufficient.

## 7. Divine death becomes possible

**Era:** Persistent consequence  
**Confidence:** Established as currently understood

Delerium, another god, or power on a comparable scale can kill a divine caretaker. Divinity no longer places a being outside fear, failure, concealment, succession, and consequence.

## 8. Dumuzi offers a cosmology of surrender

**Era:** Persistent after the Shattering  
**Confidence:** High for his expressed position

Dumuzi's case begins with real suffering. The world is broken, gods have failed, and resurrection cannot erase consequence. His answer is finality: end the conditions under which suffering can continue.

Yet Dumuzi also says, **“You must choose me.”** In the relevant bargains, choice matters, and a validly sold soul is treated as permanently bound. Free will can therefore be used to surrender ownership of the self.

## 9. Mortals inherit morally non-final gods

**Era:** All later mortal history  
**Confidence:** Structural consequence

Mortals inhabit a world where gods demonstrably exist and can help, yet can also be wrong. The landscape records their failures. Death can reveal uncomfortable truths. Ada does not routinely resolve ambiguity.

Faith therefore concerns trust, gratitude, obedience, responsibility, and judgment—not simply belief that powerful beings exist.

## 10. Roderick becomes Wrath

**Era:** Before and during 2024  
**Confidence:** High

Roderick's genuine love for Endora becomes possession. He uses a mind-swaying necklace when she wants to leave, attempts a corrupted resurrection after her death, and eventually follows Dumuzi. Personal suffering becomes claimed permission to spend strangers.

Wrath's answer to **Whose sacrifice?** is effectively: **yours, because I suffered.**

## 11. Nienna retreats and Jiangshi is lost

**Date:** June 2024  
**Confidence:** High

Nienna retreats from a disaster in Gael or the Void. Jiangshi is lost. Adelia and Bitoshi blame Nienna, and Nuru reportedly calls her a coward. Gartina argues that returning would probably only have produced another unrecoverable death.

Nienna's later sacrifice matters because she was not someone who treated heroic death as an automatic virtue. Courage is not always staying; survival is not automatically cowardice.

## 12. The Gate opens for a divine coalition

**Date:** 30 July 2024  
**Confidence:** High

Four archways or runes and realm pools participate in an intentional opening involving the caretakers. Seven newcomers arrive, though their exact origins and degrees of volition remain partly unresolved.

Mortals meet the divine order directly and can observe its urgency, priorities, and limitations. The event is not proof that every later Gate spell shares the same mechanism.

## 13. Talan refuses resurrection

**Date:** 30 July 2024  
**Confidence:** High

Arjahn asks Aionia for a diamond to resurrect a companion. Talan refuses on justice grounds, connecting the death to harm toward companions and use of Death's power. Arjahn accepts the judgment.

The event makes resurrection a question of judgment and discretion rather than a neutral service. It also asks whether Talan's judgment is identical to justice merely because Justice is his entrusted domain.

## 14. Olo's Rahu secrecy tests knowledge and loyalty

**Date:** 21 September 2024  
**Confidence:** High

Olo possesses protected Rahu knowledge connected to Delerium. He wants his companions to reach the answer without simply breaking his oath. He cooperates with their investigation while preserving enough resistance to honor the commitment.

Knowledge is not merely content to unlock. Revealing it can affect belonging, agency, loyalty, and what other people become capable of doing.

## 15. Wrath falls without repairing Gael

**Date:** Late 2024  
**Confidence:** High

Saray lands the final blow against Wrath, and Dumuzi later cradles the exhausted Roderick. Gael remains damaged, dangerous, and underpopulated. Victory begins the work of consequence rather than ending history.

## 16. Gael heals through ordinary stewardship

**Date:** 24 February 2025 onward  
**Confidence:** High

Refugees need soup, bowls, spoons, housing, materials, labor, and consultation. Wreckage can become shelter. Hope, Stake, and Shadowangel Forest make recovery visible.

Gael supplies the mortal counter-model to catastrophic divine stewardship: attention, consent, food, ecological care, practical knowledge, and accumulated work.

## 17. Aria makes divine failure politically personal

**Era:** 2025, culminating 6 November  
**Confidence:** High with unresolved biographical details

Aria's childhood suffering and failed Talan-associated prayer contribute to a pursuit of power strong enough to prevent helplessness. Her later life as Pride contains violence, rule, attachment, humiliation, and genuine relationships that do not erase one another.

Talan kills her before a tidy conclusion. The caretaker of Justice ends the life of someone whose formative wound was already bound to failed divine authority. The title of Justice does not automatically settle the meaning of that act.

## 18. The Abyss and Kurayami demonstrate collection

**Date:** January 2026 and later revelations  
**Confidence:** Events high; origin unresolved

Olo must choose under impossible pressure and selects Wren over Aravil because of the Bag. Aravil is taken as a trophy. The Abyss commands **“Olokun. Die.”** Olo dies.

Arjahn resurrects Olo using the Scepter of Faith Olo had previously given him. A prior act of generosity becomes the direct condition of return. Resurrection deepens history instead of erasing it.

## 19. Hope becomes a metaphysical answer

**Era:** 2026  
**Confidence:** Broad history high; ontology incomplete

Through Mya, Gael, Nienna, Adelia, Trees and Shards, and the survival of damaged places, Hope becomes structurally opposed to Death's claim that suffering should have the final word.

Hope does not guarantee survival, victory, fairness, or rescue. It preserves possibility: tomorrow is not owned by today's suffering.

## 20. Adelia binds sacrifice, Hope, and stewardship

**Date:** 2026  
**Confidence:** Broad form high

Adelia gives herself to restore or protect the Hope-related god-tree and becomes part of a larger tree-associated or demigod existence. The exact sequence remains incomplete.

The moral distinction is that Adelia chooses to spend herself. Her later power remains a stewardship test rather than proof that sacrifice made every future choice correct.

## 21. The Miracle of Fenumion preserves contingent grace

**Date:** 2026  
**Confidence:** Event established; ultimate cause restrained

The Miracle allows characters to experience wonder or grace without the archive declaring every improbable rescue direct intervention by Ada. If chance is converted into a divine receipt, genuine uncertainty and faith collapse into bookkeeping.

## 22. Elenia approaches the gods' old precipice

**Era:** 2024–2026  
**Confidence:** High

Elenia grows from wonder into extraordinary resurrection-scale, divine, Gate-related, and landscape-altering power. Her central question becomes:

**Will Elenia become a caretaker without becoming an owner?**

Her danger is benevolent certainty: **I can help → I know better → therefore I may choose for you.** That is the moral precipice on which the original caretakers failed.

## 23. Magnus joins resurrection, ambition, and belonging

**Era:** 2025–2026  
**Confidence:** High

Magnus repeatedly returns from death, binds himself to Dumuzi, seeks leverage, and remains sincerely attached to Zarathian culture. His choices make resurrection political and cultural rather than merely mechanical.

When he restores the memory Namo'o removed from Papirak, truth becomes another stewardship problem. Possessing the ability to recover knowledge does not automatically create the right to force another person to bear it.

## 24. Babel-Ashur reveals an exhausted cosmic front

**Date:** 5 May 2026 onward  
**Confidence:** High and partial

Mya appears battered. Arjahn's Vanguard is losing. The Shard does not fit familiar Tree assumptions. Survivors, seeds, hunts, and encroaching monsters show a region ground down rather than cleanly conquered.

Babel-Ashur demonstrates that shattered regions do not necessarily share one metaphysical implementation and that mortals inherit divine-scale responsibilities without divine-scale certainty or endurance.

## 25. Dumuzi, Kurayami, and Velkyn reopen old history

**Date:** September 2026  
**Confidence:** High for statements; provenance preserved

Later testimony returns to the primordial catastrophe through Dumuzi's indictment, Kurayami's gifts, blood, service, and ownership, and the destruction of the Abyss's cavern kingdom.

A villain can tell substantial truth. The archive neither rejects the account because of its speaker nor accepts it without comparison to evidence the speaker omits.

## 26. The Chain and Maw confront divine ambiguity

**Date:** September 2026; exact day unresolved  
**Location:** Isolated land; name unresolved  
**Confidence:** Conditions established; identity and purpose unresolved

A Delerium Chain suppresses power, and dangerous constructs defend or repair it. The Maw consumes magic and exerts tremendous pull. A figure appears trapped within; another may be watching. The name Derya Thorne has been recovered without a secure identity or allegiance.

The evidence places Talan, Delerium, imprisonment, and divine fear inside one problem without explaining their relationship. Ithilrûnë produces incompatible theories and ultimately admits ignorance. Sildithas responds by returning to an oath of mercy, hope, flourishing, joy, beauty, and courage. He proposes a feast: a small good that preserves the people facing an unresolved horror.

## Recurring questions

- **Whose sacrifice?** Nienna or Adelia choosing a cost differs from a stronger being selecting the victim.
- **Does power create permission?** Structurally no, though gods and mortals repeatedly test the boundary.
- **What may a caretaker do?** The question applies to gods, rulers, scholars, healers, guardians, and powerful adventurers.
- **What does love permit?** Love is real and still does not authorize possession.
- **Is knowing the truth enough?** Information can preserve choice or violate it depending on stewardship.
- **What does Hope promise?** Possibility, not guaranteed rescue.
- **Can the gods be trusted?** Sometimes and in some things; capacity is not infallibility.
- **Where is Ada?** Ada provides a metaphysical floor without becoming the automatic explanation for every fortunate event.

## Safeguards

1. Keep theories, testimony, interpretation, and structural fact distinct.
2. Never make a caretaker identical to the moral principle entrusted to them.
3. Preserve both divine responsibility and Death's enabling role in the original catastrophe.
4. Never treat resurrection as a reset.
5. Never make Hope equivalent to guaranteed rescue.
6. Do not assume every Tree, Shard, Gate, Delerium manifestation, or region works identically.
7. Preserve unfinished mysteries, especially Derya Thorne, the Maw, the observing figure, the Chain, and Tree/Shard relationships.
8. Keep soup, bowls, gardens, taverns, refugees, roads, harvests, feasts, and friendship attached to cosmic history.
