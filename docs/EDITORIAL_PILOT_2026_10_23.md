# Editorial pilot and wider quotation selection · 23 October 2026

This is Phase 1 of the owner’s editorial brief, plus the separately requested quotation expansion. Prose changes stop at the eight entries below. No broader rewrite is authorized by this pilot.

## Scope and method

The pilot changes 23 paragraphs, three short descriptions and two matching summary fields. It keeps the histories in place, including disagreement, failed actions, exact dialogue, dates, source references, evidence metadata, existing section destinations, relationships and protected boundaries. No lore is added to make a sentence more literary. The map/header improvements completed before this brief remain; this editorial work adds no redesign.

Original selected quest messages and surrounding scenes were read across early Pristinia, the library, Rahu fighting, Zarathian conversations, memory loss, the underwater investigation and later Hope scenes. The Temple of Secrets RP supplies Anky’s promise. Existing longer histories and supplied dossiers are compared where practical. This is a representative source review, not a claim to reread the entire archive. Quest narration outranks ambient RP; quoted claims do not become canon.

## Eight-entry pilot

### Melian Starguard

**Already effective:** Her practical civic work and the abduction/funeral chronology already give a clear identity.

**Before:** Melian survives into Pristinia’s later history. The details of her captivity and return remain incomplete, but she is not reduced to “the NPC who was kidnapped.” She becomes a continuity figure who witnesses the Rahu crisis, the growth of adventurer factions, the destruction and rebuilding of institutions, Papirak’s death, and the accumulation of a town’s memory.

**After:** Melian survives into Pristinia’s later history, although the details of her captivity and return remain incomplete. She witnesses the Rahu crisis, the growth of adventurer factions, the destruction and rebuilding of institutions, and Papirak’s death. Her later public work continues the history interrupted by her kidnapping.

**Deliberately preserved:** The kidnapping, Herb/Lilian, rite, staff custody, reported release and uncertain office remain.

**Evidence:** Selected early quest scenes and the supplied civic/funeral histories; no missing captivity scene is invented.

### Aria / Pride

**Already effective:** The childhood, flirtation, cultural authority and conflict over Scribonia are already distinct.

**Before:** Losing her therefore changes the balance of authority, memory and expectation without proving that Bowene has newly acquired his title or succeeded to a single office she occupied. The people who trusted her must confront the loss of a leader whose influence exceeded a formal title.

**After:** Her death leaves Zarathis without a leader whose influence reached beyond a formal office. Bowene had already been High Lord; her death does not establish that he acquired his title then or succeeded to an office she held.

**Deliberately preserved:** Her brother and painful denial, cultural leadership beside Bowene, coercion, achievements, death and later disputed claim remain.

**Evidence:** Explicit owner decisions govern sibling/cultural authority; existing quest scenes and their qualifications remain authoritative.

### Magnus Niriin

**Already effective:** The rapid Rahu realignment and warnings to former companions already resist a simple villain portrait.

**Before:** Magnus’s choice is not emotionally clean. While invisible and already working with the Rahu, he secretly warns Brianna that a bridge is trapped, tells her to warn the others, and asks her not to reveal him as the source. He is neither secretly still on Pristinia’s side nor incapable of personal concern. The contradiction is more consequential: he can care about people while choosing causes and structures that endanger them.

**After:** While invisible and already working with the Rahu, Magnus secretly warns Brianna that a bridge is trapped. He tells her to warn the others and conceal that he was the source. He gives a former companion a chance to avoid the trap while continuing to serve the force attacking Pristinia.

**Deliberately preserved:** His betrayal and care coexist; abduction, killings, returns, soul bargain, political ascent and individual reactions stay.

**Evidence:** The primary bridge warning is present in the quest archive, message 1376382019377827874; it does not absolve his other actions.

### Papirak the Strange

**Already effective:** The hat, hairline insults, broccoli, missing spells and quiet memory loss already sound like this person.

**Before:** His teaching was badly delivered but frequently practical: encounter a danger before facing it outside, learn what a creature can do, test a theory, and record what survives the test. Correction in training did not make every real-world failure reversible.

**After:** Papirak’s lessons asked students to encounter a danger before facing it outside, learn what a creature could do, test a theory, and record what survived the test. His shouting, impatience and confused explanations made him a difficult teacher. Being able to correct a failure in training did not make every real-world failure reversible.

**Deliberately preserved:** All direct speech, ancient identity, Gate limits, teaching/resurrection, grimoire, assault and funeral remain.

**Evidence:** The original library scene includes his expertise, missing tome, threatened spells and advice to read/write; protected history remains excluded.

### The Ale-Chemy Knights

**Already effective:** The organizational chronology, practical details, jokes and disputed rental terms already carry the faction.

**Before:** A capable crafter and a recipe are not enough. Ruben’s scouting, component hunters, transport and reliable records become necessary to make the forge useful. Ryvyt’s disappointment matters because it reveals the gap between knowing how to make equipment and possessing the resources to produce it.

**After:** Ryvyt’s disappointment came from having a recipe and the skill to use it, but lacking materials. Making the forge useful required Ruben’s scouting, component hunters, transport and reliable records.

**Deliberately preserved:** Tobias/Farkur/Ryvyt, founders’ claims, funding, accounting, criticism, absences, delivered aid and uncompleted plans remain.

**Evidence:** Existing crafting/shop evidence and original surrender dialogue are used without declaring every promised project complete.

### Voraketh

**Already effective:** The named systems, changing theories and cautious evidence boundaries are its strongest features.

**Before:** Voraketh’s attack on knowledge produced its most important institution. After losing something in the Wasteland, Anky asked Nuru for help. He said information written in the Temple’s books would remain and help explorers resist losing more “mind bits.” The Temple became protected memory, research space, scroll repository, shared equipment pool, and an inheritance for people who arrived later.

**After:** After losing something in the Wasteland, Anky asked Nuru for help. He said information written in the Temple’s books would remain and help explorers resist losing more “mind bits.” The Temple became protected memory, research space, scroll repository, shared equipment pool, and an inheritance for people who arrived later.

**Deliberately preserved:** Chains, Maw, separate figures, motes, Anky, Temple, deaths, map limits, uncertainty, latest blight/construct findings and all attributed theories remain.

**Evidence:** The Temple dialogue and refreshed quest evidence support a varied investigation; no shared explanation or final solution is supplied.

### The Common Man

**Already effective:** The people at work, food shortages, music, memorials and fire/rebuilding make it a specific tavern.

**Before:** Ordinary life is evidence. The Common Man converted world state into lived state: population pressure became dwindling fruit, labor shortage became an exhausted farmer, wealth inequality became a startling tip, and historical continuity became a returned woman recognizing the tavern where she had once been mourned.

**After:** Herb’s dwindling fruit and Farmer Frank’s exhaustion show a town feeding more mouths without enough farm labor. Lichen’s startling tip exposes the difference between adventurers’ wealth and Skye’s wages. Jiangshi later returns to the room where her memorial was held.

**Deliberately preserved:** All named workers/patrons, economics, dates, jobs, hunting caution, memorial/return, destruction, survivors and Hearth succession remain.

**Evidence:** Ordinary tavern context is checked alongside the supplied location dossier. The precise primary messages for its older harvest/tipping scene were not independently relocated.

### The Withering of Hope

**Already effective:** The sequence of prayer, return, offered gift, acceptance, attack and immediate response is already clear.

**Before:** Magnus turns toward Hope and speaks the line before using the gifted death-working power. Hope begins to wither. He then Plane Shifts toward Fein Uaill. The sentence is memorable not because it explains every motive, but because it makes the choice unmistakable: resurrection offered a renewed life, and Magnus immediately defines that life through Ambition at Hope's expense.

**After:** Magnus turns toward Hope and speaks the line before using the gifted death-working power. Hope begins to wither. He then Plane Shifts toward Fein Uaill. The attack follows immediately after the company has restored him to life.

**Deliberately preserved:** The exact declaration, divine gift, timing/order, Hope’s withering, departure, guilt and limits on responsibility/foreknowledge remain.

**Evidence:** The supplied incident reconstruction is available; its exact original scene messages and channel were not independently relocated. No higher source authority is claimed.

## Fidelity and remaining concerns

Anky and Ryvyt are explicitly marked excerpts; Camilla’s first declaration is marked as an excerpt from a longer turn. No separated passages are silently joined. Source postings retain their own date qualification and use the established Fenumion display calendar.

The older Common Man harvest/tipping sequence and the Withering’s original message locators remain recovery gaps. Their supplied dossiers support the existing account; the pilot rephrases it without adding facts or claiming a newly verified primary scene. The earlier 38-line collection is preserved, not newly certified message by message. These limitations should remain visible in future source work.

The revised pilot has more variation: Melian’s municipal work, Papirak’s unruly lessons, the Knights’ supply problems and the tavern’s people are described concretely; Aria and Magnus retain coercion and contradictory loyalties; Voraketh keeps incomplete knowledge; the Withering follows actions without a closing moral instruction. Effective humor and direct dialogue are left alone.
