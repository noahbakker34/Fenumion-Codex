# Gartina — Detailed Character Profile and History

Last synthesized: 2026-09-23

## Canon status

Gartina’s record is strongest where ordinary work intersects with catastrophe: cooking for refugees, reading dead soil, arguing for retreat, planning shelter, debating stewardship, preparing for enemies, and blaming herself when preparation fails. Her early life, complete restaurant chronology, exact role in several restorations, and later fate remain incomplete.

## Identity at a glance

- **Name:** Gartina
- **Known identity:** Halfling chef, gardener, investigator, protector, adventurer
- **Associated places:** Gael, Hope, the Tower of Gael, Pristinia, Zarathis, Gartina and Quake’s Estate
- **Important relationships:** her parents, Jiangshi, Tulaine, Dale, Draegar, Olokun, Elenia, Adelia, Nienna, Quake
- **Core method:** understand → decide → act → observe → revise
- **Model of love:** attention → curiosity → knowledge → competent care
- **Central strength:** turns concern into repeatable practical support
- **Central danger:** preparation becoming self-blame or explicit knowledge becoming arrogance
- **Primary question:** Can Gartina act responsibly before she understands enough—and accept that no amount of preparation can control every loss?

## Core character

Gartina’s central proposition is simple:

> If you care about something, learn it well enough that your care becomes competent.

She carries this principle through food, ecology, tactics, settlement work, and relationships. Tea and soup are not decorative character traits. They are methods for stabilizing people and creating enough time for thought. Gardening is not a passive love of nature. It is a discipline of intervention, observation, failure, and revision. Preparation is not merely efficiency. It is how she tries to keep other people from paying avoidable costs.

The virtue has two shadows. First, Gartina can treat knowledge that lacks an explicit causal explanation as inferior, even when embodied or traditional practice works. Second, when disaster still occurs, she can translate competence into impossible responsibility: if she should have understood enough, then every loss becomes something she should have prevented.

## Origins: cooking, gardening, and learned attention

Gartina was a gardener and cook before the better-documented Gael history.

Her parents attended the restaurants where she worked, from poor establishments to increasingly ambitious cuisine. They repeatedly told her that they were proud and wanted her to reach her potential. Gartina later describes loving them, though not at all times or in every way. Both are dead by the time of the Zarathis garden conversation.

Their pattern of support appears to have taught her that love is not a single declaration. It is repeated attention:

`show up → notice the work → learn what matters → return → help growth continue`

Her cooking history also contains failure. She learned through spectacular mistakes, reading, practice, observation, and trying again. That experience becomes the model she later applies to ecosystems and people:

`try → fail → observe → learn → try again`

The dates, locations, restaurant names, and circumstances of her parents’ deaths remain unresolved.

## Chronological history

### 12–13 June 2024 — Gael and the dead soil

The expedition chain that reaches Gael includes a ship and freed crew, an island-scale moving turtle, a Great Tree seed, Death’s dragons in the Void, and arrival at Titan’s Watch.

Titan’s Watch contains ruined Titan statues, a decayed but beautiful cathedral, and lifeless terrain. The place is framed as evidence of what happens when Death defeats a land. Gartina reads the dead soil as the stakes made physical. The observation is central to her later history: cosmic defeat is visible as an ecological condition that will require long, practical repair.

### June 2024 — Defending Nienna’s retreat

After Jiangshi is lost, Nienna is ashamed of retreating. Adelia and Bitoshi blame her, and Nienna reports that Nuru called her a pathetic coward.

Gartina rejects the simple heroic maxim that an ally must never be left behind. She argues that returning would probably have killed Nienna too and left both bodies unrecoverable. Refusing retreat can create another pointless death.

This is an early and important ethical marker. Gartina values care, loyalty, and courage, but she does not treat dying beside someone as automatically more moral than surviving an impossible rescue.

### Late 2024 — Wrath’s defeat and Elenia’s return

During the 21 November assault on Wrath, Elenia is catastrophically cut. A later New Year retrospective remembers Gartina calling Elenia back after Wrath cleaved her in two.

The exact spell, action, or shared mechanics require a primary-source pass. The memory nevertheless connects Gartina’s practical care to one of the archive’s largest battles: she is remembered not only as a combatant, but as someone trying to make another recovery possible.

Wrath is defeated around three months before 24 February 2025. Gael remains unsafe. Fiendish stragglers persist on the Plains, and liberation becomes the start of recovery rather than its completion.

### Late 2024 to early 2025 — Building life after Death

Gael’s recovery is communal. Elenia and Adelia help establish or prove a living foothold. Gartina turns toward practical restoration: contaminated soil, viable plants, remediation, seeds, pollination, succession, and how to expand life without pretending the land is already healed.

By 24 February 2025, Hope and Stake are doing well and visible from the Tower. Shadowangel Forest is new enough to require exploration. Gartina says she helped spring this changed terrain into existence.

This establishes that restoration was underway more than a year before Adelia’s later transformation. Gartina is important not because she performs the largest miracle, but because somebody must stay after a miracle and determine how tomorrow works.

### Before 24 February 2025 — Gartina remains in Gael

Adelia leaves Gael with a small group that definitely includes Elenia. Gartina does not know exactly where they went or what happened. The Tower feels noticeably quieter.

Gartina wants eventually to catch up, but remains because Gael still needs work. Refugees require care, Shadowangel Forest requires exploration, and too few new arrivals are coming from Prima to replace experienced adventurers who have departed.

Her choice is not framed as glamorous. It is continuity work: staying where need persists after attention moves elsewhere.

### 24 February 2025 — Soup, shelter, and permanent settlement

Refugees are living around the Tower in huts and shacks, supplemented by occasional magical shelter. Their ship crashed near Crustacean Cove and may be beyond repair.

Gartina worries that the refugees may feel forgotten. She and Dale prepare chicken noodle soup. They discover that the refugees lack bowls and utensils; Dale spends roughly four hundred gold pieces on bowls and spoons.

The scene then moves from immediate relief toward permanence:

- Dale proposes salvaging wood from the wrecked ship for at least a longhouse.
- Gartina suggests that Draygar may have useful materials.
- She considers scouting safer portions of the Plains for stone and metal.
- Dale asks whether anyone has actually consulted the refugees about leadership and settlement plans.
- Gartina admits that urgent survival needs had taken priority and meaningful consultation had not occurred.
- Dale goes to speak directly with the refugees about using the wreckage.

The sequence is important because competence itself must remain answerable to consent. Feeding, sheltering, and planning for people do not grant ownership of their future settlement.

### 11 August 2025 — The Zarathis garden debate

Gartina, Jiangshi, and Tulaine meet in a garden physically located in Zarathis. Gael matters as Gartina’s origin context, but the scene belongs primarily to Fein Uaill and Zarathian history.

Tulaine describes gardening and tending nature as culturally meaningful. Roughly a dozen gardeners work with the landscape around Silver Star architecture. Her approach emphasizes avoiding force, using magic only as a last resort, working with hands, minds, and hearts, reading imbalance through instinct or faith, and acting as shepherds rather than masters.

Gartina challenges the vagueness of “balance.” Cultivation is already intervention. A farmer favors crops over weeds and aphids; a grove protector draws different boundaries. No caretaker can balance an infinitely complex ecosystem through an undefined appeal to harmony.

Her demand is:

> “You’ve got to have a code.”

Gartina argues that acting without knowledge is dangerous, that caring should generate curiosity, and that love without nurturing understanding is incomplete. Her method is explicit:

`understand → decide → act → observe`

Tulaine’s method is more embodied:

`love → attend → perceive → respond`

Jiangshi asks what happens at the point of sacrifice. Is nature more important than family? Is there a person for whom one would sacrifice nature? She distinguishes being sustained or comforted by something from intentional, conscious love chosen by another person.

When discussion of Gartina’s parents makes her vulnerable, Jiangshi does not answer with theory. She places a hand on Gartina’s shoulder and remains present.

No one simply wins this conversation. Gartina correctly identifies that intervention must admit its priorities. Tulaine exposes that competent practice may contain knowledge that is difficult to reduce to theory. Jiangshi demonstrates that care can be conscious presence before it is explanation.

### Late 2025 — Magnus and political distrust

Gartina views Magnus’s claim to have changed after Pride’s death as transparently false. She also regards aspects of his political rise as suspicious. Her judgment is important character evidence but not objective proof of Magnus’s motives or electoral manipulation.

The archive should retain her wording as attributed judgment rather than turning suspicion into settled fact.

### Later history — Preparation, Olokun, and self-blame

Gartina remembers enemy tactics and assumes threats will adapt. Preparation is an expression of love: learn what happened, retain the pattern, and reduce the price paid next time.

After exhausted resources and a wrong choice contribute to Olokun’s death, this virtue turns inward. The belief that careful preparation can prevent loss becomes “I should have seen it.”

The exact date and full causal sequence of Olokun’s later death remain unresolved in the current record. The character significance is clearer: Gartina’s competence becomes a mechanism for claiming responsibility beyond what any one person could control.

### The Hallow loss and a changed identity

One preserved mechanical history records Gartina moving from an identity associated with grasslands and fields toward a protector role, losing access to Hallow in the process. The lost ability becomes biography: character mechanics record what kind of caretaker she is becoming and what earlier form of power is no longer available.

The exact date and surrounding scene should be recovered before further detail is added.

## Gartina’s philosophy of care

### Food creates the conditions for thought

Tea, coffee prepared specifically for Olokun, scones, and chicken noodle soup repeatedly bring crises down to a human scale. Food is immediate care and social infrastructure. It makes conversation, memory, and planning possible.

### Attention is an ethical act

Gartina notices when Adelia drifts, when refugees may feel forgotten, when land is contaminated, when enemies may change tactics, and when her own intervention may overstep. She watches herself think.

### Knowledge should make care competent

Good intention is not enough. Ecological systems, battles, settlements, and people have consequences that must be learned. Care should produce curiosity and disciplined revision.

### Intervention must admit that it chooses

Gardening, farming, conservation, defense, and shelter all draw boundaries. Gartina rejects the comfort of calling those choices natural or balanced without naming what is being favored and why.

### Consultation limits caretaking

The refugee settlement scene shows a necessary correction. Competent helpers can still fail to ask what the people being helped want. Dale’s question forces Gartina to acknowledge that urgent care had displaced consultation.

## Relationships

### Her parents

Their repeated attention becomes Gartina’s model of love. They showed up, watched her work change, expressed pride, and wanted her to grow. Their history explains why she links affection to curiosity and competent support.

### Jiangshi

Jiangshi complements Gartina’s explicit causal reasoning with a philosophy of intentional, reciprocal affection and embodied presence. Their relationship shows Gartina receiving care in a form that does not require analysis.

### Tulaine

The relationship is epistemic rather than simply hostile. Gartina wants explicit principles; Tulaine trusts practiced attention and instinct. Gartina can sound dismissive enough to hurt her. Tulaine’s response forces the Codex to preserve the value of knowledge that cannot always explain itself in Gartina’s preferred language.

### Dale

Dale works beside Gartina in refugee food, utensils, salvage, housing, and consultation. Their partnership moves care from immediate relief toward civic permanence while also exposing where helpers forgot to ask.

### Draegar

A community retrospective remembers Gartina and Draegar through romance as well as battle. Their relationship prevents Gartina from becoming only a philosophy or labor function. The actual chronology and development of the romance remain largely unrecovered.

### Olokun

Gartina prepares coffee specifically for Olokun, remembers tactical patterns around him, and later carries self-blame after his death. The relationship joins ordinary attention to the impossible desire to prepare well enough that a loved person cannot be lost.

### Elenia

Their shared history includes Gael’s recovery, the battle against Wrath, and Elenia’s catastrophic injury. Their paths represent complementary modes of care: Elenia’s miraculous action creates possibility; Gartina’s sustained labor makes possibility durable.

### Adelia

Gartina notices Adelia’s state, participates in the early living Gael context, and later remains after Adelia departs. Adelia’s 2026 transformation expands restoration that Gartina helped make practical; it does not erase the earlier work.

### Nienna

Gartina is one of the clearest defenders of Nienna’s retreat. She refuses to let later heroic sacrifice rewrite survival as cowardice. Her judgment preserves the moral seriousness of triage.

### Quake

The Pristinia map names Gartina and Quake’s Estate, establishing a shared geographic association. The nature and chronology of the relationship behind the estate name are not yet recovered.

### Magnus

Gartina distrusts Magnus and regards his changed-person presentation as transparent. Her testimony belongs in his political and moral history as an attributed judgment, not objective omniscience.

## Strengths

- Notices practical needs that cosmic narratives ignore.
- Converts affection into sustained labor.
- Learns from failure and revises.
- Thinks ecologically and longitudinally.
- Resists romanticized heroic death.
- Preserves tactical memory.
- Recognizes that rebuilding requires food, tools, shelter, materials, security, and consent.
- Can question whether her own help has overstepped.

## Limitations and dangers

- May undervalue intuitive, traditional, or embodied knowledge that cannot state a causal model.
- Can make other people feel that their care is unserious if they cannot explain it in her language.
- Risks converting preparation into an impossible promise that nobody will die.
- Can turn loss into personal failure through “I should have seen it.”
- Her demand for knowledge can delay action when waiting is itself consequential.
- Competent caretaking can still become paternalism when consultation is skipped.

## Supported quotations and attributed judgments
