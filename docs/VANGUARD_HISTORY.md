# The Vanguard — Recovered History

Last synthesized: 2026-09-23

## Canon status

The surviving record contains only one direct reference: on 5 May 2026, during the Babel-Ashur crisis, Arjahn is described as part of **a vanguard that is losing**.

The lowercase wording matters. It may identify a formal organization called the Vanguard, a temporary military formation, or simply a forward element in a larger force. This synthesis uses **The Vanguard** as a provisional retrieval name because that is how the subject is now being discussed. It does not promote the name to settled institutional canon.

## What is established

- A vanguard was active by 5 May 2026.
- Arjahn was part of it.
- It was losing.
- The statement appears within the Babel-Ashur crisis record.
- The surrounding crisis involved a battered Mya, severe attrition, uncertainty around Babel’s Shard, divine doubt about the number or strength of the Knights, and a choice between preparing a next wave or reuniting the existing defenders.

## Arjahn before the Vanguard

Arjahn is the only named member. His earlier history helps explain the kind of person known to have served in the formation, but it is not proof that the Vanguard existed during these events.

### 30 July 2024 — A resurrection refused

Arjahn asked Aionia for a diamond to resurrect a fallen companion. Talan refused on grounds of justice and consequence. Arjahn accepted the judgment and found reassurance in Talan’s perceived fairness.

### 21 November 2024 — The assault on Wrath

During the assault that ended with Saray’s final blow against Wrath, Arjahn offered himself. The scene places him within a recurring pattern of protective action that can become self-torturing sacrifice.

### January 2026 — Olokun’s resurrection

After the Abyss killed Olokun, Arjahn used the Scepter of Faith that Olokun had previously given him to bring him back. The act establishes Arjahn as a rescuer capable of acting decisively under catastrophic conditions.

### March 2026 — Conflict, survival, and mapping

Repeated danger and Aravil’s disappearance led Olokun to lash out at Arjahn. Arjahn defended his own agency and reminded Olokun that he also had a wife and children and understood what his risks meant. Days later, the two returned to practical work and helped preserve survival knowledge through a map.

These episodes establish soldierly discipline, rescue, route planning, concern for retreat, and a willingness to risk himself. They do not establish a Vanguard formation date, rank, or recruitment path.

## 5 May 2026 — The losing vanguard

The first and only direct Vanguard reference occurs during Mya’s account of Babel-Ashur under extreme pressure.

Mya appeared battered, and it may be that nearly everyone else had left. She insisted that all lands were worth saving. Babel’s Shard was said to be more than a tree, though its exact protective mechanism remained unknown. Within this context, Arjahn was identified as part of a vanguard that was losing.

The statement establishes activity, membership, and deteriorating circumstances. It does not establish:

- whether Vanguard was a proper name;
- who commanded the force;
- who served beside Arjahn;
- where on Babel-Ashur it operated;
- whether its objective involved the Shard;
- what losses it had suffered; or
- whether “losing” referred to casualties, territory, time, morale, or the wider war.

## The strategic fork

Mya reported that the gods believed there were too few Knights, or that the remaining Knights were too weak. She presented two possible paths:

1. Pass power and knowledge to a next wave.
2. Reunite the existing defenders and prove the gods wrong.

Possible needs included restoring Aravil and Dale, finding the Shard, and bringing strength to Babel-Ashur. The Vanguard belongs to this moment of attrition. The record does not establish which path it supported or whether it survived long enough to pursue either one.

## 29 July 2026 — Crisis without a named Vanguard

The later Babel-Ashur record says monsters were moving closer and becoming hungrier and more aggressive. An unpetrified warrior, other adventurers, and roughly a dozen natives sheltered in a cave. A seed or Shard remained central. The Knights were involved in a hunt connected to Death, and Death recruiting them appeared unlikely.

The Vanguard is not named in this later account. The July record proves that the regional crisis worsened after May; it does not prove that the cave defenders were Vanguard survivors, that the Vanguard joined the Knights’ hunt, or that Vanguard and Knights were names for the same organization.

## Conservative synthesis

The Vanguard can presently be understood only as a forward force operating under losing conditions during the Babel-Ashur crisis. Arjahn’s presence connects the formation to practical soldiering, rescue, mapping, and survival planning. The surrounding account connects it to a world running short of defenders and debating whether to preserve strength for successors or reunite the people already fighting.

Anything more specific would currently be invention.

## Questions for future source recovery

1. Was “Vanguard” a proper institutional name or a descriptive term?
2. When and why was it formed?
3. Who commanded it?
4. Who served beside Arjahn?
5. What front, route, settlement, people, or object was it defending?
6. Was its mission connected directly to Babel’s Shard?
7. What did “losing” mean in operational terms?
8. Did any Vanguard members reach the July cave refuge?
9. What was its relationship, if any, to the Knights or their hunt connected to Death?
10. Did the formation survive the Babel-Ashur crisis?
